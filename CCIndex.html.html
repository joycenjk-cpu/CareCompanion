❓ Understanding SIL — What Families Need to Know
💰 How is SIL funded?

SIL is funded through your child's NDIS plan under Core Supports — Assistance with Daily Life. It does not cover rent, food, or bills — those are paid separately via Disability Support Pension or Commonwealth Rent Assistance. SIL must be specifically approved by the NDIA.

📋 What does SIL include?

SIL support includes: personal care, cooking, cleaning, medication management, overnight and 24/7 support, life skills development, and community access. Housemate compatibility and lifestyle preferences are considered when matching residents.

🏠 SDA vs SIL — what's the difference?

SDA (Specialist Disability Accommodation) pays for the building itself — specially designed accessible housing. SIL pays for the support staff inside the home. Many people use both together. Not everyone who has SIL needs SDA.

🗺️ How do I start the SIL process?

Start by speaking with your Support Coordinator (e.g. Midwest Support Coordination). They can help prepare a Functional Assessment and Roster of Care to submit to the NDIA. Call NDIS on 1800 800 110 to discuss Home and Living supports.

💕 Caring for Yourself — Caregiver Wellbeing

You cannot pour from an empty cup. This section is for you — the parent, the carer, the one holding everything together. Here you will find what to do the moment you receive a diagnosis, how to protect your mental health, where to find support groups, and how to access respite so you can keep going.

You just received a diagnosis. What now?

The moment a doctor tells you your child has a disability is one of the most overwhelming moments a parent can experience. Whatever you are feeling right now — grief, fear, relief, confusion, numbness, love — it is valid. There is no right way to feel. And there is no perfect next step. But there are steps that help.

🚫
Step 1 — Stop. Breathe. You don't have to do everything today.

The first 24–48 hours after a diagnosis are for absorbing the news — not making decisions. Give yourself permission to just be with your child and your feelings. Nothing urgent needs to happen today.

📝
Step 2 — Write everything down from the appointment.

Write down the diagnosis, every recommendation, every referral, every name mentioned. Ask for a written copy of any letters or reports. You won't remember everything — that's normal. Ask your GP to explain anything you didn't understand.

📞
Step 3 — Call the NDIS Early Childhood line this week.

Call 1800 800 110 as soon as you are ready. For children under 6, you can access NDIS Early Childhood supports without a confirmed diagnosis. The earlier you start, the sooner support can begin. You do not need to have everything figured out to make this call.

👨‍⚕️
Step 4 — See your GP for referrals and your own mental health.

Your GP can refer your child to WACHS Child Development (08 9956 2204), therapists, and specialists. Also ask your GP for a Mental Health Care Plan for yourself — this gives you up to 10 subsidised psychology sessions per year. Your mental health matters too.

👪
Step 5 — Find one other parent who gets it.

Nothing helps more than talking to a parent who has been where you are. Ngala Geraldton (no referral needed), ConnectGroups WA, Autism WA, and diagnosis-specific Facebook groups connect you with families who understand. You are not the first parent to walk this road.

🕐
Step 6 — Take it one week at a time. Not one year.

You do not need to plan your child's entire future today. Focus on this week: one referral, one conversation, one appointment. The path becomes clearer as you walk it. Early intervention is the priority — everything else can wait.

💕 Grief is normal. Receiving a disability diagnosis for your child often triggers a grief response — not for the child you have, but for the future you imagined. This is a recognised psychological process. It does not mean you love your child any less. It means you are human. Acknowledge it. Seek support. And know that many parents describe this journey as one of the most profound and meaningful of their lives.

🧠 Caregiver burnout is real. Studies consistently show that parents of children with disability experience significantly higher rates of anxiety, depression, and stress than the general population. This is not weakness — it is the predictable result of extraordinary demands. Recognising it early and seeking help is a sign of strength and good parenting.
🔴 Signs You Need Support Right Now
⚠ Feeling constantly overwhelmed or unable to cope
⚠ Sleeping too much or not at all
⚠ Withdrawing from friends, family or activities you used to enjoy
⚠ Persistent sadness, irritability or emotional numbness
⚠ Feeling resentful of your child or your caring role
⚠ Neglecting your own health, meals, appointments
🚨 Thoughts of harming yourself or escaping — call Lifeline: 13 11 14
🚨 Feeling like your family would be better off without you — call 13 11 14
🧪 Evidence-Based Coping Strategies for Caregivers
🕑
Micro-breaks, every day

Even 10 minutes of time where you are not actively caring — a walk, a coffee, sitting in the garden — resets your nervous system. Schedule it like an appointment. It is not selfish; it is maintenance.

💬
Name your feelings — don't push them down

Research shows that naming an emotion ("I am feeling grief right now") activates the prefrontal cortex and reduces the emotional intensity. Journaling, talking to a friend, or therapy all help process feelings rather than accumulate them.

👷
Accept help — and be specific about what you need

When people offer to help, say yes. Be concrete: "Can you bring dinner Thursday?" "Can you sit with Jake for two hours Saturday?" People want to help — they just don't know how unless you tell them.

🧘
Connect with other parents who understand

Isolation amplifies grief and stress. Connecting with even one other parent who has walked this path — whether in person or online — provides perspective, practical wisdom, and the profound relief of being truly understood.

🧋
Protect your relationship with your partner

Couples raising a child with disability face additional strain. Communication, shared grief, and deliberate time together are protective. Ngala's Geraldton Family Counselling Service offers free couple counselling — no referral needed.

💪
Move your body — even briefly

Physical movement is one of the most effective evidence-based interventions for carer stress, anxiety and depression. A 20-minute walk reduces cortisol levels measurably. The free yoga sessions at Geraldton Foreshore (Wed 5:30pm, Sat 8am) are accessible and welcoming to all.

📖
Set boundaries with information overwhelm

The internet contains everything — including a great deal that will frighten and overwhelm you. Limit diagnosis research to trusted sources (raisingchildren.net.au, autism.org.au, ndis.gov.au). Choose one time of day for "research mode" — not last thing at night.

🪺
Seek professional support — it is a sign of strength

Ask your GP for a Mental Health Care Plan — up to 10 subsidised psychology sessions per year. In Geraldton: Ngala Counselling (free, no referral), Psychsense, Hamilton Clinical Psychology, In 3 Minds, Bonnie Crosthwaite. You deserve support too.

📋 How to Get Respite Funded Through NDIS
1
Ask for STA (Short Term Accommodation) — this funds overnight or weekend respite where your child stays with a provider. Must be in your child's NDIS plan.
2
Ask for in-home support hours under Core Supports — a support worker comes to your home so you can leave or rest. This is separate from STA.

If respite is NOT currently in your plan — request a plan review. Call NDIS on 1800 800 110 and explain that your caring role is unsustainable without regular respite.

Contact Carer Gateway (1800 422 737) — they can provide respite funding and support independently of NDIS, for carers who need help now.

✊ Advocacy — Know Your Rights & Find Your Voice

An advocate stands with you — or speaks on your behalf — to make sure your rights are protected, your voice is heard, and your family gets the support it deserves. All advocacy services listed here are free and independent of the NDIA and service providers.

🔍 Find an advocate instantly: Use the federal government's Disability Advocacy Finder at disabilityadvocacyfinder.dss.gov.au to search for a local advocate by postcode. Works for all of WA including Geraldton and regional areas.
What is disability advocacy?
🗣️ Individual Advocacy
An advocate works one-on-one with you to help you navigate NDIS, challenge decisions, access services, and have your voice heard in meetings, hearings or assessments.
📢 Systemic Advocacy
Organisations work to change laws, policies and systems that affect all people with disability — lobbying government, making submissions, and driving long-term change.
⚖️ Legal Advocacy
Free or low-cost legal support for discrimination complaints, NDIS appeals to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT), housing, employment and human rights matters.
💪 Self Advocacy
Learning to speak up for your own rights and your child's rights. Many organisations offer free workshops, toolkits and resources to build your confidence and knowledge.
When should you call an advocate?
✦ Your NDIS access request was rejected
✦ Your plan doesn't fund what your child needs
✦ A school refuses to enrol your child with disability
✦ A provider has mistreated or neglected your child
✦ You are being discriminated against at work
✦ You need help preparing for an NDIS planning meeting
✦ A government payment has been cut or denied
✦ You feel unsafe or your basic needs are not being met

📋 NDIS Decisions — How to Challenge Them
1
Request an Internal Review — If you disagree with an NDIS decision, you must first request an Internal Review within 3 months. Contact NDIA on 1800 800 110. PWdWA has a free Internal Review Toolkit on their website.
2
Appeal to the AAT — If the Internal Review fails, appeal to the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT). Legal Aid WA can provide free assistance with AAT appeals. Lodge at aat.gov.au.
3
Get an advocate — An advocate can support you through both the Internal Review and AAT process. They are independent of the NDIA. Use PWdWA, Legal Aid WA, or the Disability Advocacy Finder.
4
Complain about a provider — If a provider mistreats your child or violates your rights, contact the NDIS Quality & Safeguards Commission on 1800 035 544. This is separate from NDIS plan appeals.

✈️ Patient Assisted Travel Scheme (PATS) — Don't Pay Out of Pocket to See a Specialist

If your child needs to travel to Perth to see a paediatrician or neurologist, PATS subsidises transport and accommodation for eligible country WA patients. Most Geraldton families qualify.

✔️ Who qualifies: Country residents travelling 70km+ for specialist care not available locally
✔️ What it covers: Travel costs (fuel, airfare, bus) and accommodation — for patient and one escort
✔️ How to apply: Ask your GP to complete a PATS referral form at the time of specialist referral
📞 PATS enquiries: (08) 9956 1666 | health.wa.gov.au/PATS
💡 ASD Diagnosis in WA requires THREE professionals: (1) a Paediatrician — medical assessment, (2) a Psychologist with ADOS-2 — cognitive/behavioural assessment, and (3) a Speech Pathologist — communication assessment. In Geraldton, WACHS Child Development, Midwest Clinical Psychology, and All Star Therapies or AbleKids can form this team.

🗺️ Your NDIS Journey — Step by Step

Navigating the NDIS in Geraldton's Midwest-Gascoyne region. The NDIA provides direct planning services here — call 1800 800 110 to begin.

1

Get a Diagnosis & Specialist Report

Your GP can refer your child to the WACHS Child Development team at Geraldton Health Campus (08 9956 2204). A paediatrician or specialist needs to confirm the diagnosis with a written report for your NDIS application.

💡 Keep copies of all reports — you'll need them for the NDIS application and future plan reviews.
2

Check NDIS Eligibility

Your child must: (1) have a permanent disability significantly impacting daily activities, (2) be under 65, and (3) be an Australian citizen or permanent resident. For children under 6, the Early Childhood Approach applies — you don't need a formal diagnosis to start.

💡 In Geraldton, call 1800 800 110 to check eligibility. For children under 9 with disability, ask specifically about the Early Childhood partner.
3

Submit an Access Request

Call 1800 800 110 or visit myplace.ndis.gov.au to complete an Access Request Form. You'll need your child's diagnosis reports, medical information, and details about how the disability impacts daily life.

4

Attend a Planning Meeting

An NDIA planner will meet with you (in person or by phone) to discuss your child's goals and what supports they need. Come prepared with a list of current services, therapies, equipment, and what goals you have for your child's life.

💡 You can bring a support person, family member, or advocate. Midwest Support Coordination can help you prepare.
5

Receive Your NDIS Plan

Your plan will be activated in the myplace portal with funding across different support categories (Core Supports, Capacity Building, Capital Supports). Plans are typically reviewed annually or when your child's needs change significantly.

6

Choose a Support Coordinator

If your plan includes Support Coordination funding, choose a coordinator — like Midwest Support Coordination (local, Geraldton-based) — to help you find providers, set up service agreements, and implement your plan.

💡 USEA Geraldton can manage plan finances. Plan management means you can use both registered and unregistered providers.
7

Start Accessing Services

Connect with therapists (AbleKids OT, All Star Therapies), NDIS providers (Sheltering Arms, Inspire, ATLAS), and schools (Holland Street, Geraldton SHS SEN). Review your plan regularly and request adjustments if needs change.

💡 You can make a plan review request anytime if your child's circumstances change significantly.
📜 Your Rights & Policy

Understanding your rights helps you advocate effectively for your child. These frameworks span Australian, Kenyan, and global disability law — because every family, everywhere, deserves to know what protects them.

🆕 October 2024 reforms now in effect: Major changes to the NDIS Act commenced 3 October 2024. New support lists define what NDIS funding can and cannot be used for. Participants should review their plans with their Support Coordinator to ensure continued access to needed supports.
🏛️
NDIS Act 2013 (as amended 2024)
The foundational law governing the NDIS. Provides funding for "reasonable and necessary" supports. The 2024 amendment (Getting the NDIS Back on Track No.1) introduced defined NDIS support lists, total budget amounts and funding periods. Children retain the right to early intervention supports and plan reviews when needs change.
→ ndis.gov.au/governance/legislation
🧒
NDIS Early Childhood Approach
Children under 6 with developmental delay, or under 9 with disability, can access early childhood supports — even without a confirmed diagnosis. In the Midwest-Gascoyne region, the NDIA provides direct planning services. Call 1800 800 110. Supports focus on building skills during the critical early years.
→ Call NDIA: 1800 800 110
📋
NDIS Practice Standards & Code of Conduct
All registered NDIS providers must meet Practice Standards covering participant rights, provider governance, support provision, and safety. The NDIS Code of Conduct requires providers to act with integrity, honesty and transparency. Complaints about providers go to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission.
→ ndiscommission.gov.au | 1800 035 544
📚
Disability Standards for Education 2005
Schools must make reasonable adjustments for students with disability. Your child has the right to enrol in their local school AND access specialist support. Schools cannot refuse enrolment based on disability. Request an Individual Education Plan (IEP) from your child's principal. Contact the Midwest ERO on 9956 1600.
→ Contact Midwest ERO: 9956 1600
⚖️
Disability Discrimination Act 1992
Prohibits discrimination against people with disability in education, employment, accommodation, and access to services. If your child is treated less favourably because of their disability, you can lodge a complaint with the Australian Human Rights Commission — free of charge.
→ humanrights.gov.au | 1300 656 419
💰
NDIS Supports Lists (from Oct 2024)
From 3 October 2024, participants can only use NDIS funds for items on the approved NDIS Supports List. A non-NDIS supports list clarifies what cannot be claimed. A Replacement Support Rule allows limited substitutions. A 12-month transition period applies. Review your plan with your Support Coordinator urgently if you're unsure.
→ ndis.gov.au/support-lists
📝
WA Disability Services Act 1993
Western Australia's state legislation ensuring rights to access services, participate in community life, and be treated with dignity. Complements federal NDIS law. The Disability Services Commission oversees WA-funded disability services separate from the NDIS.
→ disability.wa.gov.au
🤲
Carer Recognition Act 2010
Recognises and values the role of carers — including parents of children with disability. Government agencies must recognise carers' contributions and consider their needs. Carers WA provides local support, advocacy and respite resources to Geraldton families.
→ carersaustralia.com.au | 1300 227 377
🚨
NDIS Quality & Safeguards Commission
The independent regulatory body overseeing NDIS providers across Australia. Handles complaints about provider conduct, investigates incidents, and enforces the NDIS Code of Conduct and Practice Standards. The Integrity & Safeguarding Bill 2025 further strengthens these powers. Participants have a right to complain without fear of reprisal.
→ ndiscommission.gov.au | 1800 035 544
🗣️ Advocacy support: The Disability Advocacy Service WA and Carers WA (1300 227 377) provide free support to Geraldton families. You should never have to fight alone.
🇰🇪 2025 update: Kenya enacted a new Persons with Disabilities Act 2025 (replacing the 2003 Act), strengthening alignment with the Constitution of Kenya 2010 and the UN CRPD. The National Council for Persons with Disabilities (NCPWD) is the key government body. Toll-free: 0800 724 333.
📖
Persons with Disabilities Act 2025
Kenya's new comprehensive disability law, giving effect to Article 54 of the Constitution. Strengthens the NCPWD, provides for incentives and reliefs, and establishes an institutional framework for protecting, promoting and monitoring the rights of persons with disabilities including children. Replaces the 2003 Act.
→ kenyalaw.org | ncpwd.go.ke
🏛️
Constitution of Kenya 2010 — Article 54
Guarantees the rights of persons with disability including the right to be treated with dignity, access educational institutions and facilities for persons with disabilities on an equal basis, reasonable access to all places, and use of sign language and other communication means. Article 27 prohibits discrimination on grounds of disability.
→ kenyalaw.org/constitution
🏫
Right to Education for Children with Disability
Kenyan law mandates that every learner with disability has the right to assessment and placement in an appropriate learning institution. Denying admission on grounds of disability is a criminal offence (up to 2 years imprisonment or KES 500,000 fine). The Children's Act 2022 further reinforces non-discrimination in education and healthcare.
→ Ministry of Education: kicd.ac.ke
💰
National Development Fund for PWDs
A permanent fund established to benefit persons with disabilities in Kenya. Supports organisations providing rehabilitation, and provides grants for income-generating projects and entrepreneurship support for PWDs. The Cash Transfer to Persons with Severe Disabilities (CTPSD) program provides social protection payments.
→ ncpwd.go.ke | Toll-free: 0800 724 333
👩‍⚖️
Free Legal Services for PWDs
Under Kenyan law, the Attorney-General must make regulations providing free legal services for persons with disabilities. Courts must provide free sign language interpretation, Braille services, and physical guide assistance. Persons with disabilities are exempt from certain court fees.
→ Kenya National Commission on Human Rights: knchr.org
💼
5% Employment Reservation for PWDs
The law reserves 5% of all casual, emergency and contractual positions in both public and private sectors for persons with disabilities. Employees with disability are exempt from tax on all income from employment. Employers who discriminate against qualified employees with disability face legal consequences.
→ ncpwd.go.ke
🏥
Rehabilitation & Health Services
The government must provide training facilities for speech therapy, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, guidance and counselling, audiological assessments, and aural/oral rehabilitation for children with disabilities. Education Assessment Resource Centres (EARCs) identify and place children with disabilities in appropriate institutions.
→ Ministry of Health: health.go.ke
🤝
National Council for Persons with Disabilities (NCPWD)
The government body mandated to champion rights and equalise opportunities for persons with disabilities. Coordinates provision of services, advises the Minister, registers disability organisations, and operates the National Development Fund. Based in Nairobi with county-level offices.
→ ncpwd.go.ke | Toll-free: 0800 724 333 | 020 2375994
🌍
Kenya Vision 2030 — Disability Mainstreaming
Kenya Vision 2030 recognises disability mainstreaming as a flagship project under the social pillar. Includes construction of special needs schools, affirmative action provisions, and disability-inclusive development targets. Kenya ratified the UN CRPD in 2008, making it part of Kenyan law under the Constitution.
→ vision2030.go.ke

🌐 193 countries have ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) — the most widely ratified human rights treaty in history. Both Australia and Kenya are States Parties with binding legal obligations to uphold disability rights.
🇺🇳
UN CRPD — Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006)
The world's most important disability rights treaty, adopted 13 December 2006, in force from 3 May 2008. Ratified by 193 countries. Establishes disability as a social construct arising from barriers in society, not an inherent limitation. Shifts from charity to human rights. States must consult PWDs in all policy decisions — "Nothing about us without us."
→ ohchr.org/crpd | social.desa.un.org/disability
📌
CRPD Article 7 — Children with Disabilities
States must take all necessary measures to ensure children with disabilities enjoy all human rights and fundamental freedoms on an equal basis. The best interests of the child must be the primary consideration. Children have the right to express their views freely, with support for their disability and age. Respect for evolving capacities of children with disability is a guiding principle.
→ ohchr.org/instruments/crpd/article-7
🏫
CRPD Article 24 — Right to Education
States must ensure an inclusive education system at all levels. Children with disabilities cannot be excluded from free and compulsory primary or secondary education. Reasonable accommodation must be provided, support must be given within the general education system, and effective individualised measures must be implemented to facilitate full academic and social development.
→ ohchr.org | un.org/disabilities
🏥
CRPD Article 25 — Right to Health & Article 26 — Habilitation
Persons with disabilities have the right to the highest attainable standard of health without discrimination. States must provide health services designed for persons with disabilities, including early identification and intervention for children. Article 26 requires states to provide comprehensive habilitation and rehabilitation services — including therapy for children — as early as possible.
→ ohchr.org/crpd/articles-25-26
🏠
CRPD Article 19 — Living Independently in the Community
Persons with disabilities have the equal right to live in the community with choices equal to others. States must ensure access to in-home and residential support, community services, and prevent isolation. This is the global legal basis for SIL (Supported Independent Living) and other community inclusion supports in Australia and worldwide.
→ un.org/development/desa/disabilities
🧒
UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) — Articles 2 & 23
Article 2 (non-discrimination) and Article 23 specifically address children with disability. States must recognise the right of a child with disability to a full and decent life, ensure participation in the community, and provide special care and assistance. The CRC and CRPD together establish a human right to early intervention for children with disabilities globally.
→ ohchr.org/crc | unicef.org
📊
SDGs & Disability — 2030 Agenda
The UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) explicitly include persons with disabilities across Goals 4 (education), 8 (employment), 10 (reduced inequalities), 11 (inclusive cities), and 17 (partnerships). The 2030 Agenda pledges "leave no one behind" — which specifically includes people with disabilities. Australia and Kenya are both accountable under the SDGs.
→ un.org/sustainabledevelopment | sdgs.un.org
🌍
Global Disability Summit — Commitments
First held in London in 2018 (attended by Kenya and Australia), the Global Disability Summit mobilises national and international commitments on disability inclusion. Commitments cover education, health, employment, humanitarian settings and data collection. The next Summit continues the global accountability framework for disability rights implementation.
→ internationaldisabilityalliance.org
📝
CRPD Committee Monitoring & Reporting
The UN Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities monitors implementation by all States Parties. Countries must submit regular reports on how they are implementing CRPD obligations. The Committee issues Concluding Observations and recommendations. Individuals can submit complaints under the Optional Protocol if their rights have been violated and domestic remedies are exhausted.
→ ohchr.org/en/treaty-bodies/crpd
💡 Why this matters for families: These global frameworks mean that whether your child is in Geraldton or Nairobi, they have internationally recognised rights. You can use these instruments to advocate for better services, challenge discrimination, and hold governments accountable for their commitments.
